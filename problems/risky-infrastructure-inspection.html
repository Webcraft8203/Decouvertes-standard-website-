Drone infrastructure inspection: no climbing, no shutdown

Climbing a tower or shutting down an array just to inspect it is slow, costly and dangerous.

The Problem
Who faces it

Asset owners in power, telecom, oil and gas, and heavy industry.

The main risk

Work at height is slow and dangerous, and shutdowns can disrupt operations.

What helps

A drone that gets close while the crew stays on the ground, with repeatable records.

The Problem

Why replace manual inspection with drones?

Power lines, solar plants, telecom towers and pipelines all need regular checks. Doing that by hand puts people at height and can mean taking working assets offline.

Inspecting a tower, plant or pipeline by hand can mean rope access, work at height or stopping an asset. It is slow, so inspections may happen less often than anyone would like.

A drone gets close while the crew stays on the ground. Repeat flights along the same path also build a record, so you can see how a fault changes over time.

Who It Affects

Who faces this problem

Power and telecom

Towers and lines need regular checks, often at height.

Oil, gas and pipelines

Long routes and hazardous areas make access difficult.

Industrial plants

Chimneys, roofs and structures are hard to reach during operation.

Identify It

Signs you have this problem

  • Inspections need rope access or work at height
  • Assets must stop before they can be checked
  • Reports vary depending on who did the inspection
  • You cannot easily compare this year’s condition with last year’s

Avoid These

Common mistakes

  • Collecting footage with no standard way of reporting defects
  • Not repeating the same route, so results can’t be compared
  • Flying near hazardous equipment without a site safety review
  • Expecting a drone to replace every hands-on check
Before You Decide

Questions to ask a supplier

  • What defects must we be able to see, and at what detail?
  • Is a thermal camera needed as well as a visual one?
  • How will defects be tagged and reported?
  • What do our site safety rules allow near this asset?

Questions

Common questions

What can drones inspect?

Our inspection work covers power lines, solar plants, telecom towers, pipelines and industrial assets.

Does the asset need to be shut down?

The aim is to inspect without shutdowns or rope access. Your own site safety rules decide what is allowed around live equipment.

Can a drone find faults people cannot see?

Yes. A thermal camera can show heat differences that may point to a fault, and engineers then confirm them.

How often should assets be inspected by drone?

It depends on the asset, its risk and your maintenance plan. Because flights are easier to repeat, some operators can inspect more often than before.

Who confirms what the drone finds?

Your engineers should. The drone shows where to look, and qualified staff decide what a finding means and what action to take.
