Power line inspection by drone: check long lines without climbing

Transmission lines run for kilometres across difficult land, and climbing each tower is slow and risky.

The Problem
The Problem

How do drones inspect transmission lines?

Power lines cross farmland, hills and forests. Getting a team to every tower takes time, and a close look often means climbing.

There are two common jobs. A long corridor flight records the condition of the route, while a hovering close-up looks at towers, insulators and joints.

That can call for two platforms: a VTOL fixed-wing for distance and a multirotor for detail. Keep safe distances from live lines and follow your utility’s procedures.

Identify It

Signs you have this problem

  • Line patrols take weeks to complete
  • Close checks need climbing or outages
  • Remote sections get inspected less often
  • Thermal faults are found only after failure
Choose A Solution

What to look for

  • Range for corridors, or hover for close checks
  • Thermal and zoom cameras
  • Stable flight near structures
  • Reports that tie each defect to a tower
Why It Happens

The main causes

01

Distance

Lines cover long stretches with few access roads.

02

Height

Close checks mean work at height near energised equipment.

03

Hidden heat

Failing joints may show heat before visible damage.

Questions

Common questions

Is it safe to fly a drone near power lines?

It needs trained pilots, safe distances and your utility’s approval. Electromagnetic effects and wires are real hazards.

Should I use a fixed-wing or a multirotor?

Fixed-wing suits long corridors, and multirotors suit close inspection. Our VTOL vs multirotor guide compares them.

Can a drone inspect a live line?

One aim of drone inspection is to avoid shutdowns, but your utility’s safety rules decide what is permitted.
