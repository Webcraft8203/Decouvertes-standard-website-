Swarm drones: when one drone can’t cover the area

Some jobs are too large or too risky for one aircraft, and many separate pilots are hard to coordinate.

Who faces it

The main risk

What helps

The Problem

What is a drone swarm used for?

A single aircraft has one set of eyes and one point of failure. On a very large area, or a risky mission, that may not be enough.

A swarm is a group of drones coordinated to work on one mission. Each takes part of the task, so together they cover more ground and can continue if one drops out. Swarm operations can be done with our Vision Nano and Vision 2.0.

It adds complexity, so it is not always the right answer. If one long-range aircraft can do the job, that is usually simpler.

Who It Affects

Who faces this problem

Defence teams

Several viewpoints and built-in redundancy suit large or risky missions.

Search teams

A wide area can be split into sections and searched at the same time.

Mapping teams

Dividing a large site between aircraft can shorten the time on site.

Identify It

Signs you have this problem

  • One aircraft cannot cover the area in the time you have
  • Losing a single drone would end the mission
  • You need several viewpoints at once
  • Coordinating separate pilots has become the bottleneck

Avoid These

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a swarm when one long-range aircraft would do the job
  • Planning for one operator without checking your rules and workload
  • Testing with few aircraft, then assuming it scales
  • Forgetting the extra batteries, transport and logistics
Before You Decide

Questions to ask a supplier

  • How many aircraft have been flown together, and in what conditions?
  • What happens to the task if one aircraft drops out?
  • What happens if the link between aircraft is lost?
  • What does one operator actually see and control?

Questions

Common questions

How many operators does a swarm need?

Our approach lets one operator coordinate multiple vehicles from one ground station. Your rules and mission may require more people.

Is a swarm better than one large drone?

Not always. It helps with wide coverage and redundancy, while one larger aircraft is simpler and can carry heavier sensors.

What are swarm drones used for?

Typically wide-area search, mapping and missions where redundancy matters.

What is the difference between a swarm and a fleet?

A fleet is several drones usually flown as separate aircraft. A swarm coordinates them on one mission, sharing tasks and information.

Which Decouvertes drones can fly in a swarm?

Swarm operations can be done with our Vision Nano and Vision 2.0.
