Disaster response drones: see the situation in the first hours

After a flood, earthquake or wildfire, roads and networks may be gone and nobody knows the full picture.

The Problem
The Problem

How do drones help after a flood or earthquake?

What separates a fast response from a slow one is knowing where people are and which routes are open. In the first hours, that information is often the hardest thing to get.

After a flood, earthquake or wildfire, roads may be cut and networks may be down. Decisions still have to be made, often with only scattered reports.

A drone gives command a view within minutes. It can map affected zones, search with a thermal camera and relay communications for teams on the ground.

Identify It

Signs you have this problem

  • Teams cannot reach the affected area quickly
  • Command is working from scattered reports
  • The mobile network is down or unreliable
  • You have no pre-planned aerial capability for emergencies
Choose A Solution

What to look for

  • Fast deployment from open ground
  • Thermal search-and-rescue payload
  • Mesh communications relay
  • A live shared map at the command post

Questions

Common questions

How quickly can a drone deploy in a disaster?

Our platforms are designed to deploy within minutes. Plan and practise in advance, because real response time depends on your team and location.

Can drones find survivors?

Thermal imaging can help locate people, especially against a cooler background. Ground teams still confirm and carry out the rescue.

Can drones work without a mobile network?

Yes. Flight does not depend on mobile coverage, and a mesh relay can help connect teams where infrastructure is down.
