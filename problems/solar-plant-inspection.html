Solar panel inspection by drone: find hot spots across the plant

A faulty panel can look normal, and checking thousands by hand takes too long.

The Problem
The Problem

How do drones inspect solar panels?

Solar plants can have thousands of panels across a large site. Many faults are not visible to the eye, so they can reduce output for a long time before anyone finds them.

A thermal camera shows temperature differences. A panel or cell running hotter than those around it may have a fault that needs checking.

Conditions matter: thermal inspection works best when panels are producing power under good sunlight. Engineers then confirm the flagged panels on the ground.

Identify It

Signs you have this problem

  • Plant output is lower than expected and the cause is unclear
  • Only a small sample of panels gets checked
  • Inspections take days or disturb operations
  • You have no panel-level fault history
Choose A Solution

What to look for

  • A thermal camera suited to panel-level detail
  • Automated, repeatable flight routes
  • Reports that locate each defect
  • Flights planned for good sunlight

Questions

Common questions

What faults can a thermal drone find?

It can reveal hot spots, which may indicate problems such as damaged cells, soiling or connection issues. An engineer confirms the cause.

Does the plant need to stop?

No. Thermal inspection generally needs panels to be producing power.

How often should a plant be inspected?

It depends on the plant and its maintenance plan. Repeat flights are useful because they show change over time.
