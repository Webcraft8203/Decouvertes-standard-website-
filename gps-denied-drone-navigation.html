What is GPS-denied navigation, and why do defence drones need it?

Why GPS jamming and spoofing are a risk for drones, how GPS-denied navigation keeps a UAV on course, and what to ask a supplier before you buy.

  • 29 October 2026
  • 5 min read
  • Decouvertes engineering team
Explainer

Most drones find their position from satellite signals such as GPS. That works well in open, friendly skies. In a contested area, it is the first thing an adversary will try to take away. GPS-denied navigation is what keeps a drone useful when that happens.

Why satellite navigation is a weak point

Satellite signals reach the ground very weakly, which makes them easy to disrupt. There are three common problems:

  • Jamming: a transmitter drowns out the signal, so the drone loses its position fix.
  • Spoofing: a false signal makes the drone believe it is somewhere else, so it flies the wrong way with full confidence.
  • Blockage: valleys, dense forest, buildings and indoor spaces block or reflect the signal even without an adversary.

A drone that depends entirely on GPS may drift, trigger an emergency landing or be lost. For surveillance and defence work, that is not acceptable.

How a drone navigates without GPS

GPS-denied navigation combines several on-board sensors so the drone can estimate its own movement:

  • Visual-inertial odometry: a downward or forward camera tracks features on the ground, and the motion sensors measure acceleration and rotation. Together they estimate how far and in which direction the drone has moved.
  • Sensor fusion: data from the IMU, barometer, optical-flow sensor and rangefinder are blended so that no single sensor failure misleads the aircraft.
  • Fallback logic: the flight controller detects when satellite data is missing or doesn’t agree with the other sensors, and switches to the on-board estimate automatically.
  • Return without a satellite lock: the drone can bring itself home along its recorded path instead of waiting for GPS to return.

What to ask a drone supplier

  1. What does the drone do the moment GPS is jammed? Does it hold position, continue the mission or land?
  2. Can it detect spoofing, or only a total loss of signal?
  3. How far does its position drift over a typical mission without GPS?
  4. Can it return home without a satellite lock?
  5. Has it been tested in real jamming conditions, and can they show you?

How we approach it

Our platforms use visual-inertial odometry and sensor fusion across the IMU, barometer, optical flow and rangefinder, with automatic fallback when satellite data is degraded or spoofed.
